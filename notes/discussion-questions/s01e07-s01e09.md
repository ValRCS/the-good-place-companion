# Discussion: From confession to belonging (S01E07–S01E09)

> Safe only after finishing S01E09. The grouped page refers back to all three episodes; the individual recaps preserve their tighter boundaries.

## A. Duties, consequences, and artificial persons (S01E07)

1. Is disabling Janet a harm to Janet, a wrong done by the human characters, or both? How would the answer depend on consciousness?
2. Eleanor claims to be choosing between Janet and Michael. Which omitted option reveals her own interest in framing the dilemma?
3. Compare Chidi's attitude toward the cowboy-boots compliment with Eleanor's much larger deception. Should their ethical status differ in degree, or in kind?
4. What would be an ethically defensible shutdown policy for a convincingly humanlike artificial assistant?

## B. Records, justice, and moral improvement (S01E08)

1. Separate three aspects of the peach-dress case: intentional lying, reasonably foreseeable damage, and consequential harm outside Eleanor's control.
2. Is moral responsibility backward-looking only, or should the prospect of rehabilitation matter to judgment?
3. Suppose two people perform identical good acts but one seeks only to avoid punishment. Are they morally equal?
4. Can a binary afterlife placement system evaluate a morally mixed person? What would a third category actually solve?
5. How much evidence would be needed before one could reasonably call Eleanor's newly good behavior a genuine virtue?

## C. Membership, recognition, and compassion (S01E09)

1. Why might someone repeatedly reject groups that want to include them? Is this a defense of autonomy or avoidance of vulnerability?
2. Is wanting to be admired by good people a morally worthy motive? Compare Aristotle's cultivation of character and Hegel's concept of recognition.
3. Does Michael's decision to resist Trevor express virtue or a failure to negotiate?
4. Why can Jason be empathetic toward Janet despite understanding little about formal moral philosophy?
5. What obligations does a hospitable moral community owe to an abusive guest and to its vulnerable members?

## Cross-episode debate

**Prompt:** Has Eleanor become a *good person* by the end of S01E09, or has she only become a person who *wants to become good*?

Arguments to test:

| Position | Supporting evidence by E09 | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instrumental reform | She initially studies ethics to avoid discovery | Does not explain all her later costly choices |
| Principled improvement | She confesses and accepts responsibility | A single action does not prove stable dispositions |
| Emerging virtue | She values her friends and wants to resemble them | Desire for virtue is not yet habitual virtue |

The table is an **editorial three-part heuristic**, not a fixed Aristotelian developmental schema.
