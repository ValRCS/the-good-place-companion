**Focus:** Contractualism, promises, and loyalty.

## Quick synopsis

Michael recruits Eleanor to help investigate the recurring troubles in the neighborhood. Eleanor has promised to help, but pursuing the investigation could reveal her secret. Chidi discusses contractualism while she weighs competing obligations. Eleanor attempts to divert Michael with an enjoyable day, but their growing friendship complicates her calculation. Meanwhile, Jason struggles to appear convincing as Tahani's soulmate, and Chidi is drawn into helping. Michael eventually concludes that he is responsible for the neighborhood's problems and announces that he must leave.

## Main story and character developments

- Eleanor must choose between self-preservation and a promise made to Michael.
- The characters' relationship changes as Michael and Eleanor become closer.
- Chidi helps Jason with a delicate social situation involving Tahani.

## Interpretive philosophical connections

- [Scanlonian contractualism](../../concepts/contractualism.md) asks whether a moral principle could be **reasonably rejected** by affected persons, not whether anyone may veto a rule at whim.
- This is distinct from, though historically related to, political social-contract theories associated with Locke and Hobbes.
- Unlike a straightforward sum of happiness, contractualism treats justification to each affected person as central.
