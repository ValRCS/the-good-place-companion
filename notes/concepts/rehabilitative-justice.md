# Retributive and rehabilitative justice

**In brief:** Competing or complementary aims of a system that responds to wrongdoing.

## Retributive emphasis

Retribution ordinarily asks what treatment or punishment is deserved because of a completed wrong, subject to constraints of culpability and proportionality. It need not mean cruelty or vengeance; many serious accounts explicitly reject excessive punishment.

## Rehabilitative emphasis

Rehabilitation instead emphasizes developing capacities, motivations and social conditions that enable better future choices. A genuinely rehabilitative system must ask whether growth is meaningful, whether victims' claims are acknowledged, and whether coercive “reform” respects autonomy.

## Distinctions

- **Desert:** a backward-looking assessment of what is warranted.
- **Deterrence:** attempts to prevent future wrongdoing by influencing incentives.
- **Incapacitation:** protection through restricting ability to harm.
- **Restoration:** addressing harm, responsibility and relationships, where appropriate.
- **Rehabilitation:** promoting future ethical capacity.

These aims can coexist, but they should not be conflated. A person can improve while remaining responsible for past injuries.
