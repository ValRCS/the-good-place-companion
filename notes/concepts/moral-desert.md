# Moral desert

**In brief:** The question of whether, and on what grounds, a person deserves a benefit, reward, burden or punishment.

## Explanation

Desert claims often have the form 'person P deserves treatment T in virtue of basis B'. The basis might be an action, a motive, effort, merit, or a history of choices. A theory of desert must explain which facts count and why; the mere existence of a ranking or institutional scoring rule does not establish that it measures what people morally deserve.

## Important distinctions

Desert differs from usefulness: rewarding someone may produce beneficial incentives without the recipient deserving the reward. It also differs from equality and need, which can each justify distributions independently of merit.

## A question worth debating

Can a score fairly reflect intentions, luck, resources and social circumstances? If it cannot, what remains of the claim that the resulting outcomes are deserved?
