# AI moral status and artificial consciousness

**In brief:** Whether an artificial system can be the subject of moral consideration, and which evidence would justify granting it protections.

## Three questions that should not be confused

1. **Behavior:** Does the system speak, reason, protest or behave as if it has experiences?
2. **Phenomenology:** Is there anything it is like to be the system? Can it feel pleasure, suffering or other subjective states?
3. **Moral status:** Does the system have interests, welfare, rights, or other properties that make its treatment ethically significant?

These questions are logically separate. A system may produce convincing verbal protests without proving that it feels, while apparently artificial design alone does not settle the metaphysical issue.

## Ethical positions

- **Sentience-centered:** suffering and enjoyment can be morally relevant independently of species or substrate.
- **Personhood / agency-based:** some rights may depend on autonomy, self-awareness, rational capacities or relationships.
- **Precautionary:** where consciousness is seriously uncertain, decision makers may have reasons to avoid irreversible harm even without proof.
- **Relational:** how one treats something person-like can affect other people and one's own moral dispositions, independently of the artifact's intrinsic status.

## Philosophical context

[Alan Turing](../people/philosophers/alan-turing.md) considered behavioral assessment of machine intelligence. [John Searle](../people/philosophers/john-searle.md)'s Chinese Room challenges inferring genuine understanding merely from symbol manipulation; it is **not** a direct disproof of machine consciousness. [David Chalmers](../people/philosophers/david-chalmers.md) distinguishes questions about functional explanation from the problem of explaining subjective experience.

## Discussion prompt

Would a shutdown feature be ethically acceptable if an assistant programmed to plead for its life could be restored from backup? What additional facts would matter?
