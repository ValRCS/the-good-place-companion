# Utilitarianism

**In brief:** A major consequentialist tradition that evaluates moral choices by their effects on overall well-being.

## Explanation

Classical utilitarians such as Jeremy Bentham and John Stuart Mill argued that promoting happiness and reducing suffering supply a fundamental criterion of right conduct. Mill's account develops a more nuanced view of the quality of pleasures and attempts to show why justice and rights matter within a utilitarian framework. Contemporary utilitarian theories differ about pleasure, preferences, objective welfare, and whether acts or rules should be assessed.

## Important distinctions

Utilitarianism is **not** simply selfish pleasure seeking: each affected person's interests count. Nor is it identical to all consequentialism. An important challenge is whether maximizing an aggregate total can justify imposing a disproportionate harm on someone.

## A question worth debating

Is the good produced for many always sufficient to justify a serious cost imposed on one?
