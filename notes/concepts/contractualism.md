# Contractualism

**In brief:** An approach to morality that evaluates principles by whether affected persons could reasonably reject them.

## Explanation

T. M. Scanlon's influential version asks which general principles for regulating conduct could not reasonably be rejected by people seeking principles for informed, unforced agreement. It is a theory about justification to other individuals, not a poll on whether everyone likes a rule.

## Important distinctions

**Reasonable rejection is not arbitrary veto power.** One must consider the reasons people have against candidate principles and against plausible alternatives. Scanlonian moral contractualism also differs from the political social-contract traditions of Hobbes, Locke and Rousseau, which prominently address authority, legitimacy and political association.

## A question worth debating

How should we adjudicate a conflict between one person's serious complaint and many other people's smaller conveniences?
