# Consequentialism

**In brief:** The family of theories that evaluate rightness fundamentally by how actions or rules compare in their consequences.

## Explanation

Consequentialist theories need an account of what outcomes are good, whose interests count, and how results are compared. A view could prioritize well-being, equality, knowledge or other goods. Some versions judge each act directly; others use rules or other indirect procedures.

## Important distinctions

[Utilitarianism](utilitarianism.md) is a major consequentialist theory that gives central importance to well-being. Consequentialism is broader: not every consequentialist identifies the good with happiness, and a fictional point system should not automatically be equated with either theory.

## A question worth debating

Which consequences matter, and how should we treat uncertainty and severe burdens borne by a minority?
