# Immanuel Kant (1724–1804)

German philosopher whose moral theory gives a central role to duty, autonomy, practical reason and the categorical imperative.

## Central ideas

Kant distinguishes acting *in accordance with duty* from acting *from duty*: a beneficial result alone does not settle the worth of the agent's maxim. His universal-law and humanity formulations of the categorical imperative ask whether principles can be willed universally and whether rational beings are respected as ends, not treated merely as means.

## Important distinctions

Kantian ethics is not equivalent to 'always choose what feels unpleasant' or to blindly applying a checklist. The reason for acting and respect for persons matter. *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals* (1785) and *The Metaphysics of Morals* (1797) are distinct books; the exact volume shown in S01E02 still needs checking.
