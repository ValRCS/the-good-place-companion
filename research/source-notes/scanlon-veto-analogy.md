# Scanlon's criterion versus the simplified veto analogy

**Philosophical claim:** supported by scholarship. **Episode wording:** direct timestamp verification pending.

Chidi explains contractualism through a simple example in which participants may object to proposed rules. As an introduction, that example is useful, but a free and unconditional veto is **not** T. M. Scanlon's criterion.

For Scanlon, an action is wrong when principles permitting it could be reasonably rejected by people seeking informed, unforced agreement on general principles. The strength of individual reasons, the burdens of alternatives, and mutual justification all matter. Someone's merely disliking a rule is insufficient.

This moral contractualism also differs from political social contract arguments associated with Hobbes, Locke and Rousseau. Shared vocabulary does not make the theories interchangeable.
