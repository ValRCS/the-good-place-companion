Justice as fairness

Would you accept the rules if your place in society were unknown?

The original position

Justice as fairness proposes principles for the basic structure of society. In the original position, representatives of citizens choose principles under informational restrictions: they do not know their particular class, talents, social identity or conception of the good. They do retain general knowledge about society and human needs. The device models impartiality; it is not a historical meeting or a claim that people should ignore all individual differences in everyday decisions.

The two principles

  1. An equal and adequate scheme of basic liberties for all, compatible with the same scheme for others.
  2. Social and economic inequalities must attach to positions open under fair equality of opportunity and work to the greatest benefit of the least advantaged.
  3. The first principle has priority over the second; fair equality of opportunity has priority over the difference principle. Aggregate wealth cannot justify sacrificing basic liberties.

The difference principle concerns institutional arrangements and the expectations of representative groups measured in social primary goods, not a requirement to equalize every individual's income. A maximin comparison is a teaching simplification; Rawls's argument is not that agents should always maximize the worst possible outcome.
