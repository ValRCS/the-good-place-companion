Philosophy primer

What is ethics?

Different theories can agree that choices matter while disagreeing about why.

Ethics is the philosophical study of morality, including questions about what we ought to do, how to live, and how moral claims can be justified.

Three familiar approaches

Consequentialist approaches

Evaluate actions primarily by their consequences. Versions differ on which outcomes matter and how they should be compared.

Deontological approaches

Emphasize duties, principles, rights, or constraints that need not yield simply because violating them seems to produce better results.

Virtue-ethical approaches

Focus on character, practical wisdom, and the cultivation of virtues rather than treating individual choices as the whole of morality.

Three kinds of questions

Normative ethics considers what is right and good. Metaethics examines the meaning, status, and justification of moral claims. Applied ethics investigates particular practical problems.

In real decisions, consequences, duties, and character may all matter.

Questions for viewing

  1. Are characters being judged by intentions, actions, or outcomes?
  2. Would the moral judgment change if chance altered the consequences?
  3. Which principle best explains someone's decision?
